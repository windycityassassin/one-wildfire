What "counted correctly" means

Every fire has an official outline. A fire counts as correct when one group holds at least 9 in 10 of its hot spots, and at least 9 in 10 of that group's hot spots are inside it. So there are two ways to fail:

Split. One fire, broken into pieces. This is how the 2016 rule fails .

Merged. A fire joined to other burning nearby. More room makes this more likely.

Method. The 2016 rule joins hot spots within 1.5 km and 4 days of each other (my equivalent, on hot-spot centres, of "the same 1 km cell or any of its 8 neighbours, within 4 days"). More room: 4 km and 14 days (4 km and 10 days in the Great Basin and Northern Rockies, as chosen by the left-out test). Fires with fewer than 3 hot spots are not scored. The Soda Fire (2015) is outside the 2020–2024 test; it uses 4 km and 14 days.
